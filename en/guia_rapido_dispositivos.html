Quick Reference Guide

Invasive Devices, Catheters, and Tubes

Reference guide for selection, indications, gauges, and best practices in nursing.

1. Vascular Access Devices (Intravenous and Arterial)

Device Type Gauge (G/Fr) Dwell Time Recommended Use/Flow Advantages Contraindications Main Complications
Central Venous Catheter (CVC)
 4Fr to 12Fr
(Single to Triple Lumen)		 Short-term
(Days to weeks)		 Vasoactive drugs, continuous sedation, TPN, CVP monitoring. Access to large-caliber veins. Multiple independent lumens. Severe coagulopathy, active infection at the puncture site. Pneumothorax during insertion, central line-associated bloodstream infection (CLABSI).
Peripheral Venous Catheter (Over-the-Needle Jelco/Abocath)
 14G to 24G 72 to 96 hours
(or daily clinical assessment)		 Hydration, IV medications, transfusion.
Flow: from 20 mL/min (24G) up to 240 mL/min (14G).		 Flexible plastic cannula allows mobility. Rapid insertion. Prolonged use of vesicant drugs or TPN. Phlebitis, infiltration, extravasation, and local infection.
Needle Catheter (Scalp)
 19G to 27G Immediate use
(Minutes to a few hours)		 Blood collection, rapid single-dose infusion (bolus). Fine metal needle, excellent for difficult veins for one-time access. Continuous infusions (high risk of damaging the endothelium). Rapid infiltration, vein transfixation.
PICC (Peripherally Inserted Central Catheter)
 2Fr to 5Fr Long-term
(Months to >1 year)		 Chemotherapy, prolonged antibiotics (>6 days), TPN, vesicant therapies. Inserted by nurse. Lower risk of serious insertion complications. Severe chronic renal failure (preserve venous network for fistula). Occlusion, peripheral venous thrombosis, mechanical phlebitis.
Arterial Catheter
 18G to 22G Short-term
(Days)		 Invasive Arterial Blood Pressure (IBP) monitoring, serial blood gases. Accurate continuous BP measurement in critically ill patients. Absence of collateral circulation (negative Allen's test). Distal limb ischemia, severe bleeding, thrombosis.
Implantable Venous Catheter (Port-a-Cath)
 Various sizes Very long-term
(Years)		 Oncology (intermittent chemotherapy), chronic infusions. Fully subcutaneous. Aesthetic and allows immersion baths. Patient refusal of surgical procedure, bacteremia. Extravasation due to puncture failure (Huber needle), pocket infection.
Hemodialysis Catheter (Shiley/Permcath)
 10Fr to 14Fr Shiley (Days)
Permcath (Months)		 Hemodialysis, Hemofiltration, Plasmapheresis (High Flow: >300 mL/min). Allows urgent renal replacement therapy. Use for routine medication infusion (prohibited!). Hemorrhage, severe systemic infection, central venous stenosis.
Umbilical Catheter (Newborn)
 2.5Fr to 5.0Fr Short-term
(Up to 14 days max)		 Immediate access in Neonatal ICU: blood gases (arterial) and TPN (venous). Painless for the newborn, avoids repeated peripheral punctures. Omphalitis, necrotizing enterocolitis. Lower limb ischemia, thrombosis, hemorrhage.

2. Tubes and Drains (Gastrointestinal, Urinary, and Respiratory)

Device Type Caliber (Fr) Duration Recommended Use Advantages Contraindications Main Complications
Nasogastric / Nasoenteral Catheter
 8Fr to 20Fr SNG (Days)
SNE (Weeks)		 SNG: Decompression, gastric lavage.
SNE: Exclusive enteral nutrition.		 SNE with weight and guide wire has a lower risk of aspiration (post-pyloric position). Severe facial trauma, skull base fracture. Bronchoaspiration, nasal mucosa injury and necrosis.
Indwelling Urinary Catheter (Foley 2/3 way)
 10Fr to 24Fr Days to weeks Urine output control, urinary retention. 3-way used for continuous irrigation. Balloon ensures fixation. Closed system ideal for fluid control in ICU. Pelvic trauma with suspected urethral injury/rupture. Urinary Tract Infection (UTI), urethral trauma from pulling.
Relief Urinary Catheter (Nelaton)
 8Fr to 14Fr Immediate use
(Empties and removes)		 Relief of bladder distension, collection of sterile urine sample, Post-operative. Low risk of UTI compared to indwelling catheter. Quick procedure. Acute urethral trauma, undiagnosed urethral bleeding. False urethral passage, bleeding, pain.
Suction Catheter
 6Fr to 16Fr 10 to 15 sec.
(Single use)		 Removal of secretions from upper airway or through endotracheal/tracheostomy tube. Prevention of secretion plugs and improvement of patient ventilation. Acute epiglottitis (risk of spasm), severe tracheal fistulas. Hypoxia, arrhythmias, mucosal injury, bronchospasm.
Chest Drain
 10Fr to 40Fr Until resolution of the condition Drainage of pneumothorax, pleural effusion, empyema, or hemothorax. Restores negative pressure in the pleural space, re-expanding the lung. Severe coagulopathies (assess risk/benefit). Subcutaneous emphysema, infection, drain obstruction, lung injury.

Best Nursing Practices

  • Aseptic Technique: Hand hygiene and strict skin antisepsis (alcoholic chlorhexidine is the gold standard).

  • Push-Pause Technique: Perform flush (salinization) before and after medications (turbulence system).

  • System Safety: Keep urinary systems and drains closed, airtight, and always below the insertion level.

  • Daily Inspection: Evaluate all signs of inflammation every day. If access is no longer necessary, remove it.

  • Standard Identification: Every dressing must contain: Name of the person who inserted it, date, caliber, and time.

Caliber Selection

Trauma / Emergency 14G (Orange) or 16G (Gray). Flow up to 240 mL/min.
Adult / Major Surgeries 18G (Green) or 20G (Pink). Excellent flow, lower risk.
Pediatrics / Elderly 22G (Blue) or 24G (Yellow). Preservation of venous network.

Golden Rule: The HIGHER the Gauge (G), the thinner the diameter. The HIGHER the French (Fr), the thicker the device (1 Fr ≈ 0.33 mm).
