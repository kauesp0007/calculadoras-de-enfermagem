Introduction to Neonatal Scales

Birth represents the most dramatic transition in human physiology: in minutes, the newborn moves from a liquid environment with placental oxygenation to independent pulmonary respiration. Systematic assessment of this transition is one of the most critical responsibilities of the neonatal nursing team, and the neonatal scales are the tools that make this assessment objective and standardized.

O Apgar score, created by Virginia Apgar in 1953, is the most universally known instrument in medicine. Applied at the 1st and 5th minute of life, it assesses five parameters (heart rate, respiratory effort, muscle tone, reflex irritability, and skin color) that reflect adaptation to extrauterine life. Despite its limitations — it was not designed to predict long-term neurological outcomes — it remains the gold standard in immediate newborn assessment.

A gestational age is the main determinant of neonatal morbidity and mortality. When the date of the last menstrual period (LMP) is uncertain, the Capurro e New Ballard scales allow estimation of postnatal gestational age through somatic and neuromuscular parameters. The Ballard, more complete and accurate, assesses 12 items; the Capurro, faster, uses only 5 somatic parameters.

In the neonatal ICU, assessment of pain with instruments such as CRIES is essential, as the neonate cannot verbalize its discomfort. Studies show that repeated exposure to pain in the neonatal period without adequate analgesia has consequences for long-term neurodevelopment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Ballard and Capurro?
Capurro (1978) uses 5 somatic parameters (skin texture, ear shape, mammary gland, plantar creases, and nipple formation), is faster (2-3 min) and suitable for newborns over 29 weeks. The New Ballard Score (1991) assesses 12 parameters — 6 physical (skin, lanugo, plantar surface, breast, eye/ear, and genitalia) and 6 neuromuscular (posture, popliteal angle, scarf sign, heel-to-ear, wrist flexion, and arm recoil) — being more accurate and applicable to extreme preterm infants (from 20 weeks). Ideally, it should be applied in the first 12-24h of life. In Brazilian practice, Ballard is the gold standard for neonatal ICU and preterm infants, while Capurro is often used in rooming-in for its simplicity.
What does each letter of APGAR mean?
APGAR is an acronym coined by Joseph Butterfield in 1963 in honor of Virginia Apgar: A = Appearance (skin color: cyanotic=0, acrocyanosis=1, pink=2), P = Pulse (HR: absent=0, <100=1, >100=2), G = Grimace (response to stimulus: absent=0, grimace=1, cry/cough=2), A = Activity (tone: flaccid=0, slight flexion=1, active movement=2), R = Respiration (effort: absent=0, irregular=1, strong cry=2). Total 0-10. Assessment at 1st minute guides resuscitation; at 5th minute has prognostic value.
When is Apgar considered normal?
Apgar 7-10 is considered normal, indicating good adaptation to extrauterine life. Apgar 4-6 at 1 minute indicates moderate asphyxia — usually the newborn responds well to positive pressure ventilation and oxygen, reaching 7-10 at 5 minutes. Apgar 0-3 is severe neonatal asphyxia, requiring immediate resuscitation according to the SBP Guidelines (2022): positive pressure ventilation, intubation, and cardiac massage if HR < 60. O Apgar do 5º minuto é mais preditivo que o do 1º — Apgar 0-3 no 5º minuto está associado a maior mortalidade (até 50%) e risco elevado de paralisia cerebral.
