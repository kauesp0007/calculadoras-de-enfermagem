Introduction to Perioperative Scales

O perioperative comprises three distinct phases — preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative — each with specific assessment needs. The perioperative nursing uses validated scales to systematize the assessment of surgical risk, prevention of adverse events, and safe discharge from the Post-Anesthesia Care Unit (PACU).

A ASA Classification (American Society of Anesthesiologists), created in 1941 and regularly revised, is the universal system for assessing preoperative physical status. It classifies the patient into 6 categories (ASA I to VI) based on the presence and severity of systemic diseases, and is an independent predictor of perioperative morbidity and mortality. ASA should be determined by the anesthesiologist during the pre-anesthetic consultation and recorded in the medical record and the safe surgery checklist.

In the intraoperative period, the ELPO assesses the risk of injuries from surgical positioning. In the PACU, the Aldrete and Kroulik Index is the gold standard for assessing post-anesthetic recovery and determining eligibility for discharge from the PACU. It assesses 5 parameters: motor activity, respiration, circulation, consciousness, and O2 saturation. Aldrete ≥ 9 indicates discharge conditions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the ASA classification?
ASA I: healthy patient, non-smoker, no comorbidities. ASA II: mild controlled systemic disease (e.g., controlled hypertension, BMI 30-35, smoking). ASA III: severe systemic disease with functional limitation (e.g., poorly controlled diabetes, COPD, BMI > 40, dialysis-dependent CKD). ASA IV: severe systemic disease with constant threat to life (e.g., sepsis, cardiogenic shock, recent MI). ASA V: moribund patient, not expected to survive > 24h without surgery. ASA VI: declared brain death (organ donor). The letter "E" can be added (e.g., ASA IIE) for emergency surgeries, which have 2-3x higher risk.
