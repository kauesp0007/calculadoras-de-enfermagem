Introduction to Nursing Management

O nursing staff sizing is one of the most critical responsibilities of the nurse manager. An undersized team compromises patient safety and increases rates of adverse events, hospital infections, and professional burnout. An oversized team, on the other hand, generates inefficiency and waste of resources. The balance depends on an objective assessment of the workload — and that's where the Patient Classification Systems (PCS).

In Brazil, the COFEN Resolution No. 543/2017 and the COFEN Normative Opinion No. 01/2024 establish the minimum parameters for sizing the nursing staff. The calculation uses as a basis the PCS that classifies patients into care categories: minimal, intermediate, high dependency, semi-intensive, and intensive. Each category corresponds to a minimum number of nursing hours per patient/day.

A Fugulin Scale and the Perroca Classification are the two main PCS used in Brazil for this purpose. Fugulin assesses 9 care areas; Perroca classifies 12 critical indicators. Both are recognized by COFEN and widely used in public and private hospitals.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Fugulin and Perroca?
Both are valid PCS. Fugulin (1998, HU-USP) assesses 9 care areas: mental status (1-4), oxygenation, vital signs, motility, ambulation, feeding, body care, elimination, and therapeutics — each scoring from 1 to 4, totaling 12 to 48 points. Perroca assesses 12 indicators with scores from 1 to 5 each (12-60 points). Fugulin is the most used for sizing according to COFEN Resolution 543/2017. COFEN Opinion 01/2024 establishes the times: 4h (minimal), 6h (intermediate), 10h (high dependency and semi-intensive), 18h (intensive).
