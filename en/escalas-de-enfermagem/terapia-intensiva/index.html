Introduction to Intensive Care Scales

A Intensive Care Unit (ICU) is the hospital environment where the most critically ill patients are concentrated, with imminent risk of death and need for continuous monitoring and complex interventions. In this context, assessment scales are indispensable tools to quantify severity, predict outcomes, guide therapeutic management, and optimize resource allocation.

Unlike scales used in other specialties—which usually focus on a single organ system—ICU scales are often multisystem, reflecting the complex nature of the critically ill patient, who may present simultaneous dysfunctions in multiple organs.

O APACHE II (Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation) is the most well-known severity score worldwide. Applied in the first 24 hours of admission, it uses 12 physiological variables, age, and comorbidities to estimate the risk of hospital mortality. The SOFA (Sequential Organ Failure Assessment) assesses dysfunction of six organ systems daily, currently being the main criterion for sepsis definition according to the Sepsis-3 consensus: an increase of 2 or more points in SOFA, associated with infection, defines the septic condition.

In addition to severity scores, the intensive care nurse uses scales to delirium detection (CAM-ICU), neurological assessment in sedated or intubated patients (FOUR, Glasgow), pain assessment in non-verbal patients (BPS — Behavioral Pain Scale) and severity stratification in respiratory infections (CURB-65).

The systematic application of these scales, integrated with the Nursing Care Systematization (SAE), allows early detection of clinical deterioration, accurate communication during multidisciplinary rounds, and objective evaluation of response to therapeutic interventions.

Gold standard for severity assessment in the ICU. Uses 12 physiological variables, age, and chronic diseases. Predicts hospital mortality. Score 0-71.

Confusion Assessment Method for ICU

Detection of delirium in critically ill patients under mechanical ventilation. 4 features: fluctuation, inattention, disorganized thinking, level of consciousness.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the SOFA score and how to interpret it?
The SOFA (Sequential Organ Failure Assessment) evaluates dysfunction of 6 organ systems: respiratory (PaO₂/FiO₂), coagulation (platelets), hepatic (bilirubin), cardiovascular (MAP and use of vasopressors), neurological (Glasgow), and renal (creatinine or urine output). Each system scores from 0 to 4, totaling 0 to 24. An increase of 2 or more points from baseline, associated with suspected infection, defines sepsis according to Sepsis-3. The higher the score, the higher the mortality — studies show that SOFA ≥ 11 is associated with mortality above 80%.
What is the difference between APACHE II and SOFA?
APACHE II is applied only once within the first 24 hours of ICU admission and uses the worst values of 12 physiological variables, age, and presence of chronic diseases to predict hospital mortality. It is a "snapshot" of severity at admission. SOFA, on the other hand, is calculated daily, allowing monitoring of the evolution of organ dysfunction over time — it is a "movie" of the critically ill patient. Furthermore, SOFA is the defining criterion for sepsis (Sepsis-3), while APACHE II is primarily a prognostic score. In practice, both are complementary: APACHE II estimates baseline risk and SOFA monitors response to treatment.
How to apply CAM-ICU in a mechanically ventilated patient?
CAM-ICU is applicable in mechanically ventilated patients. First, assess the RASS (Richmond Agitation-Sedation Scale). If RASS = -4 or -5 (deep coma), stop — the patient is not eligible. If RASS ≥ -3, proceed: (1) Acute change or fluctuating mental status — compare with baseline; (2) Inattention — letter test (squeeze hand upon hearing "A" in the sequence S-A-V-E-A-H-A-A-R-T) or picture test; (3) Disorganized thinking — ask 4 simple questions and ask to raise 2 fingers; (4) Altered level of consciousness — RASS different from 0. Delirium is diagnosed when features 1 + 2 + (3 or 4) are present. Assessment should be done every shift.
