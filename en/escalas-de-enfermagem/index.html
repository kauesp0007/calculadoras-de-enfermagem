What are Nursing Scales

The nursing scales are standardized clinical assessment instruments, built from scientific evidence, that allow nurses to transform subjective observations into objective and measurable data. They constitute one of the pillars of Nursing Care Systematization (SAE), providing reliable parameters for each stage of the nursing process.

In investigation (nursing history), the scales guide systematic data collection, ensuring that no relevant aspect of the patient's condition is neglected. In nursing diagnosis, the scores obtained help identify pertinent NANDA-I diagnoses. In planning, they support the prescription of NIC interventions proportional to severity. In evaluation, they allow monitoring the patient's evolution objectively and comparably over time.

Beyond direct care application, nursing scales have importance administrative and managerial. Instruments such as the Fugulin Scale and the Perroca Classification support nursing staff sizing, as determined by COFEN Resolution No. 543/2017. Scales such as Braden, Morse, and Downton feed quality of care indicators and patient safety programs.

The routine use of validated scales is recognized by organizations such as the World Health Organization (WHO), the Joint Commission International (JCI) and the Federal Nursing Council (COFEN) as a standard of good practice. However, choosing the appropriate instrument requires technical-scientific knowledge: each scale was developed for a specific purpose and its application out of context can lead to misinterpretations.

This guide brings together the main scales used in Brazilian and international nursing, organized by clinical specialty, with comparative tables, decision flowcharts, and direct access to the project's interactive calculators.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are nursing scales?
Nursing scales are standardized and scientifically validated instruments used to assess, classify, and monitor patients' clinical conditions. They transform subjective observations into objective data, allowing systematization of care and precise communication among health professionals.
What are the most used nursing scales?
The most used scales in clinical practice include: Glasgow Coma Scale (neurological assessment), Braden Scale (pressure injury risk), APACHE II and SOFA (ICU prognosis), Fugulin Scale (care complexity classification), Morse Scale (fall risk), Aldrete Scale (post-anesthetic recovery), and the Numerical Pain Scale.
What is the importance of scales in SAE?
Scales are fundamental for SAE because they provide objective data for each stage: in investigation, they allow standardized data collection; in diagnosis, they help identify NANDA-I diagnoses; in planning, they guide the prescription of NIC interventions; and in evaluation, they allow monitoring the patient's evolution in a measurable and comparable way.
How to choose the correct scale for each patient?
The choice depends on the patient's clinical profile, the care context, and the assessment objective. For neurological patients, Glasgow or FOUR is used; for skin assessment, Braden or Waterlow; for fall risk, Morse or Downton; for pain, numerical scale or FLACC in non-verbal patients. The nurse should select the most appropriate validated instrument for the situation, considering the psychometric properties and the population for which the scale was developed.
