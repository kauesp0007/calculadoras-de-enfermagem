Introduction to Obstetric Scales

A obstetric nursing occupies a privileged position in the maternal-child care line: it is the link between prenatal care, childbirth, and initial newborn care. The obstetric scales are instruments that systematize clinical assessment in these three critical moments, supporting decisions that directly impact maternal and neonatal morbidity and mortality.

O Bishop Score, described by Edward Bishop in 1964, is the quintessential obstetric scale. It assesses five characteristics of the cervix — dilation, effacement, consistency, position, and fetal station — to predict the likelihood of successful labor induction. A favorable Bishop score (> 8 points) is associated with a vaginal delivery rate of 80-90%, while an unfavorable Bishop score (< 6) sem maturação cervical prévia frequentemente resulta em cesariana. As diretrizes da FEBRASGO e do ACOG recomendam a aplicação sistemática do Bishop antes de qualquer indução.

At the moment of birth, the transition to immediate neonatal care is mediated by the Apgar score. Although classified as a neonatal scale, its application at the 1st and 5th minute of life is inseparable from obstetric care — the obstetric nurse is responsible for the initial assessment of the newborn and must communicate the Apgar score to the team as an indicator of fetal-neonatal transition.

The scales of Ballard e Capurro complete the picture by allowing the estimation of postnatal gestational age, essential information for classifying the newborn (preterm, term, or post-term) and for defining clinical management.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Bishop Score and how to interpret it?
The Bishop Score assesses cervical conditions to predict the success of labor induction. The 5 parameters: cervical dilation (0=0 cm to 3=≥5 cm), effacement (0=0-30% to 3=≥80%), consistency (0=firm to 2=soft), position (0=posterior to 2=anterior), and fetal station (0=-3 to 3=+2). Total score 0-13. Bishop < 6: colo desfavorável — recomenda-se maturação cervical antes da ocitocina. Bishop 6-8: intermediário. Bishop > 8: favorable — direct induction with oxytocin, high probability of vaginal delivery.
What is the relationship between Bishop and mode of delivery?
Bishop is the main predictor of successful vaginal delivery induction. Bishop > 8: vaginal delivery success rate of 80-90% with oxytocin alone. Bishop < 6 sem maturação: 40-50% de falha de indução, resultando em cesariana. A FEBRASGO recomenda maturação cervical (misoprostol 25 mcg vaginal a cada 6h ou sonda de Foley) quando Bishop < 6. Após maturação, reavalia-se o Bishop — se ≥ 6, inicia-se ocitocina. Bishop persistentemente desfavorável após maturação e membranas rotas geralmente indica cesariana.
How does Apgar relate to obstetric care?
Apgar reflects both the newborn's condition and the quality of obstetric care. Obstetric factors that influence it: high epidural anesthesia can depress the newborn (transient low Apgar), instrumental vaginal delivery (forceps/vacuum) has worse Apgar than normal delivery, prolonged labor and fetal distress reduce Apgar, elective cesarean without labor generally has higher Apgar. The obstetric nurse must communicate the Apgar to the team and document it as part of the partograph and integrated assessment of the mother-baby dyad.
