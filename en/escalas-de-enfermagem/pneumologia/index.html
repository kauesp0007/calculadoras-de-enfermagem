Introduction

The respiratory infections — particularly community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) — are among the leading causes of mortality from infectious diseases worldwide. The decision between outpatient treatment, hospital admission, or ICU admission has a direct impact on patient survival, and severity stratification scales such as CURB-65 are tools that make this decision more objective and evidence-based.

O CURB-65, developed by Lim et al. in 2003 from the analysis of more than 1,000 patients in three countries, assesses 5 simple criteria: Confusion, Urea, Respiratory Rate, Blood Pressure, and Age ≥ 65 years. With a score from 0 to 5, it stratifies the risk of 30-day mortality and guides the appropriate level of care. CURB-65 is recommended by the guidelines of the Brazilian Society of Pulmonology and Phthisiology (SBPT) and the British Thoracic Society (BTS).

When a respiratory infection progresses to sepsis, the scores SOFA (Sequential Organ Failure Assessment) and APACHE II (Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation) become the reference instruments for monitoring organ dysfunction and predicting mortality in the ICU.

Frequently Asked Questions

How to interpret CURB-65?
CURB-65: C = Confusion (1 point if disoriented), U = Urea > 50 mg/dL (1), R = Respiratory Rate ≥ 30 breaths/min (1), B = Blood Pressure — systolic < 90 ou diastólica ≤ 60 mmHg (1), 65 = Idade ≥ 65 anos (1). Total 0-5. 0-1: baixo risco (mortalidade < 3%), tratamento ambulatorial seguro. 2: risco intermediário (mortalidade ~9%), considerar internação. ≥ 3: alto risco (mortalidade 15-40%), indicação de internação em UTI. A versão CRB-65 (sem ureia, 0-4 pontos) pode ser usada na atenção primária.
