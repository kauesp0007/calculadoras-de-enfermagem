Introduction to Pain Assessment

A pain has been recognized as the 5th vital sign since the 1990s, and its systematic assessment is a patient's right and a nursing team's duty. However, pain is a subjective and multidimensional experience — making its measurement a challenge, especially in populations that cannot communicate verbally.

In conscious and communicative adult patients, the Numeric Pain Scale (0-10) is the gold standard: simple, quick, and with excellent correlation with pain intensity. But what when the patient is a pre-verbal child, a newborn, um elderly with advanced dementia or is under mechanical ventilation in the ICU? For each of these situations, there are validated behavioral scales.

O FLACC (Face, Legs, Activity, Cry, Consolability) is the most versatile behavioral scale — applicable from infants to elderly with cognitive impairment. The CRIES was developed specifically for neonatal postoperative pain. And the BPS (Behavioral Pain Scale) is the instrument of choice for critically ill patients under mechanical ventilation and sedation.

Adequate pain assessment is the first step toward effective management. The Brazilian Society for the Study of Pain (SBED), the International Association for the Study of Pain (IASP), and COFEN recommend systematic documentation of pain intensity as a quality-of-care indicator.

FLACC

Behavioral pain scale for children aged 2 months to 7 years and non-verbal patients. 5 items, score 0-10. Broad international validation.

Neonatal Postoperative Pain

Scale for assessing postoperative pain in newborns. 5 parameters: crying, FiO2, HR/BP, facial expression, and sleep. Score 0-10.

Frequently Asked Questions

How to assess pain in an intubated ICU patient?
The BPS (Behavioral Pain Scale) is the recommended scale for mechanically ventilated patients. It assesses: facial expression (relaxed=1, tense=2, grimace=3, grimace with closed eyes=4), upper limb movements (absent=1, partial flexion=2, full flexion with finger flexion=3, permanent flexion=4), and adaptation to mechanical ventilation (tolerant=1, coughs but tolerates=2, fights the ventilator=3, unable to control=4). Total score 3-12. BPS > 5 has 80% sensitivity for significant pain and indicates need for analgesia. Assessment should be performed before and after painful procedures to monitor analgesic efficacy.
Which pain scale to use in newborns?
For term newborns: CRIES (Crying, Requires O2 for SpO2 > 95%, Increased vital signs, Expression, Sleepless) for postoperative pain — score 0-10, each item 0-2. NIPS (Neonatal Infant Pain Scale) for acute procedures (0-7). For extreme prematures (< 32 semanas): PIPP (Premature Infant Pain Profile) que considera idade gestacional corrigida, FC, SpO2 e 3 expressões faciais. A escolha depende da idade gestacional e do contexto (dor aguda vs. dor mantida). A avaliação deve ser feita a cada 2-4 horas e sempre após intervenções analgésicas.
What does FLACC mean and when to use it?
FLACC stands for Face (0=neutral to 2=frowning grimace), Legs (0=relaxed to 2=kicking), Activity (0=quiet to 2=rigid), Cry (0=no cry to 2=intense cry), and Consolability (0=comfortable to 2=difficult to console). Each item 0-2, total 0-10. It is the most versatile behavioral scale: originally validated for children aged 2 months to 7 years (Merkel, 1997), but widely adapted for any non-verbal patient — elderly with dementia, patients with cognitive impairment, ASD, post-stroke with aphasia. FLACC 0 = no pain; 1-3 = mild; 4-6 = moderate; 7-10 = severe. It should be applied at rest and during procedures.
