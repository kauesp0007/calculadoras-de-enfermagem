Rapid Response Team (RRT)

Within the scope of PlanificaSUS and Patient Safety, the Rapid Response Team (RRT) is a vital strategy designed to prevent clinical deterioration of patients admitted outside the Intensive Care Unit (ICU).

The central premise is that cardiorespiratory arrest (CRA) is rarely a sudden event; it is often preceded by hours of physiological instability (changes in vital signs) that, if detected and treated early, can be reversed.

Implementation

The implementation of the RRT aims to achieve critical hospital safety goals:

  • Reduce the occurrence of Cardiorespiratory Arrests (CRA) in inpatient units (Code Blue).
  • Decrease overall hospital mortality.
  • Reduce unplanned ICU admissions, or ensure that when necessary, they occur under better clinical conditions.
  • Support frontline nursing staff with immediate specialized support.

Structure of the Rapid Response System

The system is not just a team, but a process composed of two "arms" and a management component:

Afferent Arm (Detection and Activation)

It is continuous surveillance. It involves the inpatient unit staff (nurses and technicians) identifying warning signs.

Tools

Use of early warning scales such as NEWS for adults or PEWS (Pediatric Early Warning Score) for children.

Action

Upon identifying a "trigger" (scale score or altered vital sign), the team activates the team.

Efferent Arm (Response)

It is the team itself that responds to the call.

Typical Composition

Physician (usually intensivist or hospitalist), Intensive Care Nurse, and Physiotherapist.

Ação

Immediate bedside assessment, patient stabilization, and decision on disposition (keep in the unit with new management or transfer to ICU).

Administrative Component (Management and Quality)

Data monitoring for continuous improvement.

Indicators: Number of calls, response time, CRA rate per 1,000 discharges, hospital mortality.

4. Activation Criteria (Common Triggers)

The manual highlights that activation should be objective (data-based) or subjective (staff concern).

Warning Vital Signs (Examples)

Parameter Activation Criterion (Example)
Airway Threat of obstruction, stridor
Breathing RR < 8 or > 28 bpm; O2 Saturation < 90%
Circulation HR < 40 or > 130 bpm; SBP < 90 mmHg
Neurological Decrease in mildl of consciousness (Glasgow), seizure
Others Urine output < 50ml/4h; Staff concern ("The patient doesn't look well")

Note: "Code Yellow" is often used for clinical deterioration, while "Code Blue" is reserved for confirmed CRA.

Steps for Implementation (PlanificaSUS Roadmap)

  • Awareness: Engage the board and care teams.
  • Protocol Definition: Choose the scales (MEWS/NEWS) and define the call flow.
  • Training: Train ward teams to recognize severity and "have permission" to call for help.
  • Infrastructure: Ensure equipment (emergency cart, defibrillator) and communication system (pager, dedicated phone).
  • Improvement Cycles (PDCA): Evaluate the calls and adjust process failures.

Conclusion

The RRT changes the hospital culture from "reaction to chaos" to "proactive prevention". In the context of PlanificaSUS, it integrates care, ensures that the patient receives the right attention at the right time, and empowers the nursing team through clear communication and support criteria.
